    How the picture becomes music

    The image is reduced to a 64 by 32 grid. Each cell gets a color, a texture score, and a "salience" score (how different it is from the image's average). The playhead reads one column per eighth note. Only cells that stand out get a note, so calm ocean stays quiet and hot spots speak up.

    PictureSound
    Vertical positionPitch, quantized to a musical scale. North is high, south is low.
    Horizontal positionTime, and stereo pan. West is left, east is right.
    Cell hueBase instrument. Warm: saw pad. Green: pluck. Blue: glass bell. Purple and gray: soft choir. The Studio Mixer can remap these notes to orchestra, solo strings, guitars, or scanner pings.
    How much a cell stands outLoudness. Cells close to the image average are silent.
    SaturationBrightness of the tone (filter opening).
    Smooth or busy textureSmooth: long notes. Busy: short, and the hi-hat joins.
    Average hue of the whole frameKey and mode. Warm: Dorian. Green: Mixolydian. Blue: Lydian. Purple: Aeolian.
    Overall amount of detailBase tempo, from 64 to 118 BPM. A record's documented artistic preset may adjust it.
    Column brightnessThe low drone gets louder and opens its filter.

    Using the notes of a scale is the artistic rule that keeps raw pixels from turning into noise. Every mapping is deterministic: the same frame always makes the same music.

    What comes from the image: note position, loudness, brightness, texture, key, and base tempo are calculated from the pixels. What comes from artistic direction: each NASA track has a documented preset that limits instruments and adjusts density, drums, drone, tempo, and reverb to make its Earth story easier to hear.

    This is an accessible artistic interpretation of rendered EIC imagery, not a measurement instrument. Labels, coastlines, legends, and other graphic elements in a frame can also affect its sound. Follow the NASA source link for the underlying science and authoritative data description.

    Imagery: NASA Scientific Visualization Studio, from the Earth Information Center content database. Built for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, "The Earth Information Jukebox". NASA does not endorse this project.
